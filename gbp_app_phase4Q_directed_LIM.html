Your Problem

The virtual-particle dial D: exchanging a particle of mass μ between two charges produces V(x) = −g·e−μ|x|/√(x²+a²) — force range = 1/μ, the mediator's Compton wavelength. μ = 0 is the photon: Coulomb, infinite range. Crank μ up and watch the bound states below get shallower and die — that's why the weak force is short-ranged (W/Z are heavy) and electromagnetism is not. This is Yukawa's 1935 argument that predicted the pion.

Allowed: numbers, x, + - * / ^ ( ), sin cos tan exp log sqrt abs. Example: 0.5*x^2, 5*(abs(x)<1), exp(-x^2)

How This Works

D textbook quantum mechanics

Punch in your problem and get the propagator K(xᵇ,T; xᵃ,0) = ⟨xᵇ|e−iHT/ℏ|xᵃ⟩, the amplitude to go from a to b in time T. Natural units ℏ = 1. When Feynman’s analytic propagator exists (free particle or harmonic oscillator), the app uses that exact result immediately. Otherwise, it runs split-operator time evolution and removes the finite-source bias with selectable Richardson extrapolation. Steep confining potentials automatically use a fourth-order Yoshida integrator. Bound-state energies come from a separate Schrödinger diagonalization.

Where GBP fits: everything on this tab is standard textbook QM — the continuum path integral. The experimental coprime-lane and Catalan constructions are kept together on the Viz / Scattering tab so their arithmetic and their physical interpretation cannot be confused with this textbook solver.

1. Why the i keeps the wave bounded

iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = Hψ  →  ψ(t) = e−iEt/ℏψ(0)

This is Schrödinger’s equation. The i is the entire reason quantum amplitudes rotate rather than grow — it turns the exponential from a real number (which explodes or dies) into a phasor spinning on the unit circle. Norm is exactly conserved. The spinning dot on the right is e−iEt/ℏ running in real time.

D

2. From Schrödinger to Feynman — what the path integral sums

K(xᵇ,T; xᵃ,0) = ∫𝒟x(t) eiS[x]/ℏ  —  every path gets phase eiS/ℏ, paths near the classical one add coherently, others cancel

Feynman (1948) showed Schrödinger’s equation is equivalent to summing eiS/ℏ over every possible path from xᵃ to xᵇ. The i in the exponent is the same i as above — each path contributes a unit phasor, not a real weight. Paths near the classical trajectory (δS = 0) have nearly the same phase and add up. Random paths have random phases and cancel. The propagator on the Path Integral tab computes this sum numerically.

3. Select a modulus — watch which lanes survive interference

ZN* = {r : gcd(r,N)=1}  —  the coprime residues mod N; φ(N) lanes survive destructive interference

Colored ticks are the coprime lanes — they complete the circle exactly once and survive; gray lanes hit sub-cycle resonances and cancel by destructive interference. At N = 30, φ(30) = 8 lanes survive — matching the 8 gluons of QCD.

The lane-coloring selector is now explicit about what is chirality. Euler C1/C2 is the chirality assignment; T1 stroke parity and half-period are retained only as geometry comparisons:

  • Euler C1/C2 — χ₋₄ (default): C1 for r ≡ 1 mod 4 and C2 for r ≡ 3 mod 4. This is the ± sign that drives the Euler/Dirichlet-beta product in panel 4.
  • T1 stroke parity: the 720° double-cover geometry used by the mass-model visualization. It remains available for comparison, but the donut test does not use it to assign C1/C2.
  • half-period sign: r < N/2 vs r > N/2, the sign of sin(2πr/N). Also a comparison coloring, not the C1/C2 selector.

For the lift/cancellation test below, C1/C2 is fixed to Euler χ₋₄ even if you temporarily recolor this toroid with T1 or half-period. That prevents the visualization selector from changing the physics hypothesis being tested.

Odd moduli are flagged red. An odd N has no factor of 2, so it cannot carry the 720° spinor double cover at all — which is why 15, 21, 25 and 27 are not viable toroid moduli even when their lane counts look right.

The wave ring is sectioned by angle: pick a lane r and the inner ripple draws ψₖ(θ) = sin(rθ) around the toroid — red where positive and blue where negative. The gold dot samples the wave one lattice step in (θ = 2π/N); squaring that signed sample gives P(r). Interpreting this finite lane picture as the path-integral measure remains a GBP hypothesis, not the textbook Schrödinger solver used on the Calculator page.

3b. Another Damn Donut 🍩 — lift / impedance test

Start with the surviving lanes of a base modulus B, make q lifted copies r+jB, then test which candidates still survive at N=Bq. If q adds only primes already present in B, the pattern is a pure repeat. If q introduces a new prime, some lifted phases are punctured. C1/C2 is fixed to the Euler χ₋₄ chirality: C1 = +1 (r ≡ 1 mod 4), C2 = −1 (r ≡ 3 mod 4). The test then pairs mirror lanes and checks whether C1 + C2 cancels with equal Malus weight.

Try the comparison pair 30 x 4 = 120 (pure mod-30 repetition) versus 24 x 5 = 120 (five candidate lifts with new-prime filtering). Same final N, different decomposition.

3c. Lane Interaction Matrix (LIM)

Pair every surviving lane with every other surviving lane and measure directional overlap. This is the formal version of the ADD experiment: the donut discovers the basis; LIM turns that sparse basis into a vector-to-vector interaction matrix.

Free/core LIM: pairwise overlap heatmap, Malus amplitude/power weighting, Euler C1/C2 signing, pair inspector, and the numerical matrix. More general matrix rearrangements or decomposition tools can be added separately without changing this physical-lane view.

4. Euler / Catalan product — primes sort into two chirality classes

G = β(2) = ∏p odd prime (1 − χ−4(p)/p²)−1 ≈ 0.9159… (Catalan’s constant)

Every odd prime falls into one of two classes: p≡1 mod 4 (χ = +1, gold, split) or p≡3 mod 4 (χ = −1, blue, inert) — the standard distinction in the Gaussian integers Z[i]. This χ₋₄ classification is separate from both the half-period sign and T1 stroke parity.

The lower trace is the product being built one prime at a time, starting from the correction factor: gold (split) primes push it up, blue (inert) primes pull it down, and the oscillation closes on G. Changing N moves the starting point and crosses out the primes dividing N — and the trace still lands on the same true G at every modulus, because the correction exactly compensates whichever primes were skipped.

This makes the doubling structure visible: N is only felt through its odd-prime support, so moduli sharing that support give a pixel-identical trace — N = 60 is mod-30 run twice (φ(60) = 16 lanes = the Z₃₀* pattern at r and r+30; correction 15/16 for 15, 30 and 60 alike), and N = 24 is mod-12 twice (Z₁₂* at r and r+12; correction 9/10, shared with 9, 12, 27, 36). The subtitle names the moduli in each class.

N = 15 is the minimum modulus that contains both a split prime (5) and an inert prime (3) as factors — both classes simultaneously present in the correction. Below 15: N = 12 has only 3 (pure inert); N = 10 has only 5 (pure split). N = 30 = 2×15 adds the prime 2 (the ramified prime in Z[i], the only one that neither splits nor stays inert) to get φ(30) = 8 lanes. The correction factor 15/16 at N = 30 is exact. See: Gaussian Prime Physics paper (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21050302) for the full derivation.

Throughout the papers, every claim is labeled: (D) derived and verified, (H) hypothesis, (P) falsifiable prediction. No claim here competes with QED’s 12-decimal-place precision. The claim is structural: that 30 = 2×3×5 forces the gauge group, not the other way around.

A note on the mathematics

Sophus Lie (1842–1899) built Lie algebra to classify symmetries by zooming into the identity transformation and reading off the tangent vectors there. His machinery works perfectly once you have a smooth continuous group — and the Standard Model runs on Lie groups.

What Lie algebra cannot do is tell you which Lie group physics uses. SU(3) has 8 generators and describes 3 colors, but “why N = 3” is a question Lie theory accepts as input, not output.

Z₃₀* is a finite discrete group — no tangent space, no smooth manifold, nothing to differentiate. φ(30) = 8 is pure number theory. The claim is that the discrete structure selects the Lie group, and the continuous Lie machinery takes over from there. Ramanujan wrote down mock theta functions in 1920 with no idea they would describe black hole entropy a century later. Euler’s totient function is from the 1700s. The pieces were always there.
